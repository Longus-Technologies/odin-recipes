Pilau

Description

Pilau is a flavorful rice dish that is popular in many cultures around the world. It is typically made with rice, spices, and a variety of ingredients such as meat, vegetables, or legumes. The specific ingredients and preparation methods can vary widely depending on the region and cultural influences.

In some versions of pilau, the rice is cooked with aromatic spices such as cumin, cardamom, cinnamon, and cloves, which give the dish its distinctive flavor. The rice is often cooked in a broth or stock, which adds depth and richness to the dish. Pilau can be served as a main course or as a side dish, and it is often accompanied by a variety of toppings or garnishes such as fried onions, nuts, or fresh herbs.

Ingredients

For the Beef:
  • 500 g beef (boneless, cut into small cubes)
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 3-4 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger, gratedSalt and pepper to taste
For the Pilau Spices:
  • 1 tbsp cumin seeds
  • 1 tbsp cardamom pods
  • 1 tsp whole cloves
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 2 tbsp pilau masala (store-bought or homemade)
For the Rice:
  • 2 cups Basmati rice (washed and soaked)
  • 4 cups liquid (beef broth + extra water if needed)
  • 1/4 cup cooking oil or ghee
  • 2 onions, thinly sliced
  • 2 tbsp garlic & ginger paste

Instructions

  1. Boil the Beef

    2. In a large pot, place the cubed beef, chopped onion, crushed garlic, grated ginger, and a pinch of salt. Add about 1.5 to 2 cups of water, cover, and boil until the beef is tender. Drain the beef and save the leftover broth (stock)! You will use this for cooking the rice.

  2. Toast and Grind the Spices

    3. In a dry frying pan, lightly roast the cumin, cardamom, cloves, and cinnamon for 1-2 minutes until highly fragrant. Grind these whole spices into a fine powder and mix them with your pilau masala.

  3. Caramelize the Onions

    4. Heat the oil or ghee in your heavy-bottomed pot over medium heat. Add the thinly sliced onions and fry slowly until they turn a rich, deep brown. Do not let them burn, but get them dark—this is the secret to the deep color and flavor of the pilau.

  4. Sauté Aromatics and Meat

    5. Stir in the garlic-ginger paste and your blended spices, cooking for about 1 minute until fragrant. Toss in the boiled beef chunks and coat them thoroughly in the spice mixture.

  5. Add Rice and Broth

    6. Pour in the washed, soaked basmati rice and gently stir to coat the grains with the spices and oil. Measure out exactly 4 cups of liquid (using the same cup you used for the rice). If your beef stock is less than 4 cups, top it up with hot water. Pour this into the pot, stir once, and taste the liquid—it should be slightly saltier than you normally prefer so the rice absorbs the right amount of flavor.

  6. Cook and Steam

    7. Bring the mixture to a boil. Once most of the water has evaporated and you start to see small "craters" or bubbles in the rice, reduce the heat to the absolute lowest setting. Cover the pot tightly (you can use foil under the lid to trap steam) and let it cook for 15-20 minutes.

  7. Serve

    8. Turn off the heat and let the pilau rest, covered, for 10 minutes. Gently fluff the rice with a fork and serve hot, ideally with Kachumbari (a fresh tomato and onion salad) and pilipili masala.
