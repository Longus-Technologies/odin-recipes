Ugali

Ugali is a staple food in many African countries, particularly in East Africa. It is a simple dish made from maize flour (cornmeal) and water, cooked to a thick, dough-like consistency. Ugali is often served as a side dish and is commonly eaten with various stews, vegetables, or meats.

To make ugali, maize flour is gradually added to boiling water while stirring continuously to prevent lumps from forming. The mixture is cooked until it thickens and pulls away from the sides of the pot, indicating that it is ready. The resulting ugali can be shaped into balls or served in a mound on a plate.

Ugali is a versatile dish that can be enjoyed in many ways. It can be eaten with a variety of accompaniments, such as sukuma wiki (collard greens), nyama choma (grilled meat), or fish. It is a filling and satisfying meal that provides a good source of carbohydrates and energy.
