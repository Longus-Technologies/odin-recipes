Ugali

Description

Ugali is a staple food in many African countries, particularly in East Africa. It is a simple dish made from maize flour (cornmeal) and water, cooked to a thick, dough-like consistency. Ugali is often served as a side dish and is commonly eaten with various stews, vegetables, or meats.

To make ugali, maize flour is gradually added to boiling water while stirring continuously to prevent lumps from forming. The mixture is cooked until it thickens and pulls away from the sides of the pot, indicating that it is ready. The resulting ugali can be shaped into balls or served in a mound on a plate.

Ugali is a versatile dish that can be enjoyed in many ways. It can be eaten with a variety of accompaniments, such as sukuma wiki (collard greens), nyama choma (grilled meat), or fish. It is a filling and satisfying meal that provides a good source of carbohydrates and energy.

Ingredients

  • Water: 4 cups
  • Maize Flour (Unga wa mahindi): 2 to 21/2 cups (coarse or fine white cornmeal)

Instructions

  1. Boil the water

    2. In a heavy-bottomed pot (traditionally called a sufuria), bring the water to a rolling boil over high heat.

  2. Add initial flour

    3. Pour a small handful (about 1 tablespoon) of the maize flour into the boiling water.

  3. Add the rest

    4. Gradually and steadily pour in the rest of the maize flour while continuously stirring with a flat wooden spoon (mwiko) to prevent lumps from forming.

  4. Mix and mash

    5. As the mixture thickens into a heavy porridge, continue stirring. Press the mixture against the sides of the pot to break up any stubborn lumps.

  5. Shape and cook

    6. Reduce the heat to medium-low. Gather the ugali into a large, smooth dome or mound in the center of the pot. Let it cook covered for 3-5 minutes so it solidifies and a light crust forms at the bottom.

  6. Serve

    7. Carefully flip the ugali, remove the pot from the heat, and use a wet spoon or flat bowl to shape it into a perfectly rounded loaf. Turn it out onto a serving plate.
